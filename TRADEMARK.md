**Stellora is free to use, modify and self-host. The "Stellora" name, logo and
official visual identity remain reserved to the original project.**

The MIT License (see [LICENSE](LICENSE)) grants you freedom to use, modify, and
redistribute the **code**. It does **not** grant you any right to use the
**"Stellora" name, logo, or visual identity** as your own product.

## What you may do

- Fork the code and modify it for your own use.
- Self-host and run your own instance privately.
- Build derivative works and publish them — under a **different name**.

## What you may NOT do

- Distribute a modified or rebranded version **under the name "Stellora"**.
- Use the Stellora logo, wordmark, or official visual style to present your own
  product as if it were the original.
- Imply official affiliation with or endorsement by the Stellora project.

## Acceptable naming for forks

| ✅ Allowed | ❌ Not allowed |
|---|---|
| GalaxyCore | Stellora |
| MyGalaxy | Stellora 2.0 |
| Stellar Explorer | Stellora by \<you\> |
| Nebula Notes | The real Stellora |

## Examples

- Original project: **Stellora** — *Original Project*
- Your fork: **GalaxyCore**, **MyGalaxy**, **Stellar Explorer** — *a fork of Stellora*

This policy exists so people can freely build on the code while the "Stellora"
name keeps meaning *the original project*. It is a trademark/brand reservation,
not a technical restriction — the software ships no license-key or phone-home
check.

If you want to use the Stellora name or brand officially, contact the project
owner first.
